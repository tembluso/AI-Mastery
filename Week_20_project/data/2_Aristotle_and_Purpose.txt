Aristotle and Purpose

For Aristotle, every being moves toward its purpose—its final cause. Man’s telos is eudaimonia, a flourishing that arises not from pleasure but from living according to reason and virtue. Modernity often confuses motion with progress, yet Aristotle reminds us that activity without direction is emptiness disguised as effort. Purpose is not found in abundance but in alignment: to act in harmony with one’s essential nature.
